# Evidence log: stretching for runners and for calf strains

## Questions

1. Does stretching prevent running injuries (or lower-limb injuries generally)?
2. Does it improve or impair running performance / running economy (acutely and long term)?
3. Does it improve range of motion (ROM), and is it better than strength training for that?
4. Does it reduce post-exercise soreness?
5. Does stretching help recovery from a muscle strain (calf specifically)?
6. Static vs dynamic, and before vs after running.

## Search

Europe PMC: titles with stretching + runners/running + injury/performance/economy; Cochrane reviews on
running-injury prevention and stretching for soreness; systematic reviews of stretching and injury;
stretching in strain rehabilitation. Sorted by citations.

## Sources

### P1. Yeung SS, Yeung EW, Gillespie LD. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2011;(7):CD001256 — ABS
"Interventions for preventing lower limb soft-tissue running injuries." 25 trials, 30,252 participants
(mostly military recruits; three trials of general-population runners). "We found no evidence that
stretching reduces lower limb soft-tissue injuries (6 trials; 5130 participants; risk ratio [RR] 0.85, 95%
confidence interval [95% CI] 0.65 to 1.12)", "compatible with either a reduction or an increase".
Conclusion: "the evidence base … is very weak, with few trials at low risk of bias."

### P2. Pope RP, et al. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2000;32:271–277 — ABS (RCT)
1538 army recruits, 12 weeks; one 20-s static stretch for each of "six major leg muscle groups" in every
warm-up vs no stretching (the abstract does not list which muscles). No significant effect on all injuries (HR 0.95, 0.77–1.18),
soft-tissue injuries (HR 0.83, 0.63–1.09) or bone injuries. "Fitness … may be an important, modifiable
risk factor."

### P3. van Mechelen W, et al. Am J Sports Med 1993;21:711–719 — ABS (RCT)
421 male recreational runners, 16 weeks; standardized warm-up, cool-down and stretching vs control:
4.9 vs 5.5 injuries per 1000 h; "not effective in reducing the number of running injuries".

### P4. Thacker SB, et al. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2004;36:371–378 — ABS (systematic review)
"Stretching was not significantly associated with a reduction in total injuries (OR = 0.93, CI
0.78-1.11)"; "not sufficient evidence to endorse or discontinue routine stretching before or after
exercise to prevent injury".

### P5. Lauersen JB, Bertelsen DM, Andersen LB. Br J Sports Med 2014;48:871–877 — ABS (meta-analysis)
25 RCTs, 26,610 participants: "no beneficial effect for stretching (RR 0.963 (0.846-1.095))", whereas
strength training (RR 0.315), proprioception training (RR 0.550) and multiple-exposure programmes (RR 0.655)
were protective; "consistently favourable estimates were obtained for all injury prevention measures except
for stretching."

### P6. Behm DG, et al. Appl Physiol Nutr Metab 2016;41:1–11 — ABS (systematic review)
Immediately after stretching: static (−3.7%), dynamic (+1.3%), PNF (−4.4%) performance changes; "greater
performance deficits with ≥60 s (−4.6%) than with <60 s (−1.1%) SS per muscle group"; when post-stretch
dynamic activity was included "no clear performance effect was observed". "SS and PNF stretching had no
clear effect on all-cause or overuse injuries; no data are available for DS." ROM improvements "typically
lasting <30 min".

### P7. Konrad A, et al. Front Physiol 2020;11:630282 (PMC7857312) — ABS (scoping review)
11 studies, 111 participants. A single static stretch up to 90 s per muscle group: small change in running
economy (1.0%, 95% CI −1.04 to 2.22) but "negative effects were reported in running performance (−1.4%;
95% CI: −3.07 to −0.17)". Dynamic stretching: negligible change in economy, possible performance benefit
(wide CI). "Only short static stretching durations of ≤60 s per muscle-tendon unit are advisable" before
running; "less flexible runners have greater benefits from stretching" (one study).

### P8. Nelson AG, et al. Scand J Med Sci Sports 2001;11:260–265 — ABS (controlled trial)
10 weeks of 40-min, 3×/week thigh and calf stretching in active students: no change in the oxygen cost of
a submaximal run — "a chronic stretching program does not necessarily negatively influence running
economy."

### P9. Afonso J, et al. Healthcare (Basel) 2021;9:427 (PMC8067745) — ABS (systematic review + meta-analysis)
11 RCTs, 452 participants: "no differences between ST [strength training] and stretching on ROM (ES =
−0.22; 95% CI = −0.55 to 0.12; p = 0.206)"; studies highly heterogeneous.

### P10. Herbert RD, de Noronha M, Kamper SJ. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2011;(7):CD004577 — ABS
12 studies: stretching before, after, or both "does not produce clinically important reductions in
delayed-onset muscle soreness in healthy adults" (e.g., post-exercise stretching −1.04 points on a 100-point
scale at one day).

### P11. Malliaropoulos N, et al. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2004;36:756–759 — ABS (cohort, not randomized in the abstract)
80 athletes with second-degree **hamstring** strains; the group doing a more intensive stretching programme
regained normal ROM faster (5.6 vs 7.3 days) and returned to full training sooner (13.3 vs 15.1 days).
Limit: hamstring, not calf; allocation method not described in the abstract.

## What the evidence does and doesn't say (no decisions here)

- **Injury prevention:** multiple RCTs and reviews (P1–P5), including one that stretched six major leg
  muscle groups every session (P2), found no clear injury reduction from stretching. The evidence is "very weak" overall (P1)
  and compatible with small benefits or harms.
- **Performance:** long static stretches (≥ 60 s per muscle) right before effort cause small performance
  drops (P6, P7); short static stretches (< 60 s) or dynamic stretching, followed by dynamic activity, have
  little effect (P6). Ten weeks of regular stretching did not harm running economy (P8).
- **Range of motion:** stretching increases ROM (P6, acutely; P9, long term), and strength training
  through full range improves ROM about as much (P9).
- **Soreness:** stretching does not meaningfully reduce soreness (P10).
- **Strains:** the only stretching-in-rehab evidence found is for hamstring strains (P11, faster ROM recovery
  and return with more stretching). No calf-specific stretching trial was found; calf rehab guidance is in
  `calf.md`.
- Not found: a trial showing any specific stretch reduces calf strains in runners.
