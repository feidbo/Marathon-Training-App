# Evidence log: dumbbell / bodyweight replacements for the machine routine

## Question

The current routine uses machines: leg press, single-leg calf raise on the leg press, hip thrust machine,
hip abductor machine, seated leg curl (plus an assisted pull-up). Which dumbbell or bodyweight exercises
load the same muscles? (An assisted pull-up stays optional; the user asked for no other machines.)

## Search

Europe PMC: titles combining gluteus medius/maximus, hamstring, soleus/gastrocnemius/heel raise,
split squat/Bulgarian/unilateral with EMG/activation/training, sorted by citations; systematic reviews
preferred, then laboratory and training studies.

## An important limit on this kind of evidence

### X1. Vigotsky AD, et al. Front Physiol 2017;8:985 (PMC5758546) — ABS
Comparing surface-EMG amplitudes between exercises is common, and researchers "hypothesize about possible
longitudinal adaptations, such as strength and hypertrophy. However, such conclusions are frequently
unsubstantiated and unwarranted." → EMG rankings below show which muscles an exercise works, not proof
that one exercise builds more strength than another.

## Quadriceps / glutes (replacing the leg press)

### Q1. Speirs DE, et al. J Strength Cond Res 2016;30:386–392 — ABS (RCT, training outcome)
18 academy rugby players, 5 weeks, 2×/week at matched relative loads: rear-foot-elevated split squat only
vs back squat only. Both improved 1RM back squat and 1RM split squat; "No significant interactions between
group and time"; "BI and UNI training interventions may be equally efficacious in improving measures of
lower-body strength". Limit: small, short, young male athletes.

### Q2. Andersen V, et al. Int J Sports Med 2014;35:1196–1202 — ABS (EMG)
15 resistance-trained men at 6RM: Bulgarian (rear-foot-elevated split) squat vs squat — "similar
activations in the vasti muscles"; Bulgarian squat had greater biceps femoris activity (63–77%) and lower
rectus femoris (16–21%).

### Q3. Neto WK, et al. J Sports Sci Med 2020;19:195–203 (PMC7039033) — ABS (systematic review, EMG)
16 studies; "very high level of GMax activation (>60% MVIC)" for step-ups and their variations, split
squat, lunges, hip thrust variations, modified single-leg squat, deadlifts. "The step-up exercise and its
variations present the highest levels of GMax activation".

## Calves (single-leg calf raise)

### C1. Hébert-Losier K, et al. J Strength Cond Res 2012;26:3124–3133 — ABS (EMG)
48 adults, unilateral heel raises at 0° and 45° knee flexion: soleus activity "4% greater" at 45°, and
gastrocnemius "5% lower"; consistent with knee-straight (gastrocnemius) and knee-bent (soleus) heel raises,
"however, the 4-5% documented change in activity might not be enough to significantly influence clinical
outcome measures or muscle-specific benefits."

### C2. Kovács B, et al. Biol Open 2024;13:bio061810 (PMC11708772) — ABS (EMG + dynamometer)
30 men: knee-flexed vs knee-extended plantarflexion — torque 31% lower and medial gastrocnemius EMG 35%
lower with the knee bent; soleus EMG 15–28% higher in the first half of slower contractions with the knee
bent.

### C3. Hébert-Losier K, et al. J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 2011;41:505–513 — ABS
Single-leg heel-raise test with knee straight vs ~30° bent: people hold the intended angles on average but
individual angles vary widely; "Clinicians should, therefore, interpret select heel raise test outcomes
with caution".

(Calf-raise loading for the strained calf is covered in `calf.md`.)

## Gluteus maximus (replacing the hip thrust machine)

- Q3 (Neto 2020): barbell/band hip thrust variations and step-ups, split squats, lunges, single-leg squats
  and deadlifts all "very high" (> 60% MVIC) gluteus maximus activation.

### G1. Distefano LJ, et al. J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 2009;39:532–540 — ABS (EMG, 21 people, 12 exercises)
"The best exercise for the gluteus medius was side-lying hip abduction, while the single-limb squat and
single-limb deadlift exercises led to the greatest activation of the gluteus maximus."

## Gluteus medius (replacing the hip abductor machine)

- G1 (Distefano 2009): side-lying hip abduction best for gluteus medius among 12 exercises.

### G2. Boren K, et al. Int J Sports Phys Ther 2011;6:206–223 (PMC3201064) — FT (EMG, 24 people, 18 exercises)
Gluteus medius > 70% MVIC: "side plank abduction with dominant leg on bottom (103%MVIC), side plank
abduction with dominant leg on top (89%MVIC), single limb squat (82%MVIC), clamshell (hip clam)
progression 4 (77%MVIC), and front plank with hip extension (75%MVIC)." Authors note values above 100%
may reflect sub-maximal reference contractions or co-contraction.

### G3. Reiman MP, et al. Physiother Theory Pract 2012;28:257–268 — ABS (literature review)
Describes gluteal activation across rehabilitation exercises and the role of moment arm, gravity and
positioning in exercise design (no single ranking in the abstract).

## Hamstrings (replacing the seated leg curl)

### H1. Llurda-Almuzara L, et al. Int J Environ Res Public Health 2021;18:8733 (PMC8393607) — FT (systematic review, EMG)
29 studies, 507 participants, 114 exercises. Biceps femoris long head: Nordic and isokinetic categories
highest (> 60% MVIC); hip thrust 53.5%, leg curl 48.8%, deadlifts 42.2%; glute bridges 32.3%, squats
30.2%. Bodyweight "slip leg" (a sliding exercise) 99% and "heel strike against ball" 94% in one study.

### H2. Hegyi A, et al. Scand J Med Sci Sports 2019;29:34–43 — ABS (HD-EMG, 19 athletes, 9 exercises)
"Straight-knee bridge, upright hip extension, and leg curls exhibited the highest hamstrings activity" in
both phases (eccentric 40–54%, concentric 69–85% MVIC). Exercises included slide leg curl and unilateral
Romanian deadlift.

### H3. van den Tillaar R, et al. Int J Sports Phys Ther 2017;12:718–727 (PMC5685404) — ABS
12 men; hamstring exercises reached 18–75% of sprinting EMG; Nordic variations and the "laying kick" were
highest; cranes lowest.

## Not found / limits

- No study compared these dumbbell/bodyweight exercises against the specific machines with *training*
  (strength-gain) outcomes in runners; the evidence is mostly acute EMG (see X1), plus one small
  unilateral-vs-bilateral training RCT (Q1).
- Nordic hamstring curls need the ankles anchored (partner or fixed object).

## Addendum, Nov 14 2026 — is there a dumbbell exercise that trains the pull-up muscles?

Searched Europe PMC (titles/abstracts) for EMG and training studies on rows, pullovers, pull-downs and
pull-ups. ABS = abstract read; FT = full text read.

### L1. Di Fonza D, et al. J Funct Morphol Kinesiol 2026 (PMC13510307) — FT (narrative review, 23 EMG studies)
- Covers lat pull-down, pullover, seated row, barbell row, bent-over row and pull-up. No dumbbell-row study
  is among the 23.
- "the pullover appeared to preferentially activate the pectoralis major rather than selectively target
  the LD."
- Among rows, the inverted row had the highest latissimus amplitude, but from one study of 7 men (L2),
  "preliminary pending replication".
- Findings "reflect acute EMG amplitude rather than longitudinal training outcomes".

### L2. Fenwick CM, Brown SH, McGill SM. J Strength Cond Res 2009;23:350–358 — ABS (EMG, 7 men)
- Inverted row vs standing bent-over row vs standing one-armed cable row. "The inverted row elicited the
  highest activation of the latissimus dorsi muscles, upper back, and hip extensor muscles" with the
  lowest spine load. The bent-over row "produced large activation symmetrically across the back but
  produced the largest lumbar spine load."

### L3. Marchetti PH, Uchida MC. J Appl Biomech 2011;27:380–384 — ABS (EMG, 8 men)
- "the barbell pullover exercise emphasized the muscle action of the pectoralis major more than that of
  the latissimus dorsi".

### L4. Li Q, et al. Eur J Sport Sci 2025 (PMC11667758) — ABS (RCT, 34 men, 8 weeks)
- Both groups trained only the lat pull-down; pull-up repetitions improved significantly in both. Shows a
  different pulling exercise can carry over to pull-ups — but it is a vertical pull, not a row.

### What was not found
- No EMG or training study of the **one-arm dumbbell row** specifically (one search hit, unrelated).
- No trial testing whether any row (dumbbell or otherwise) improves pull-up performance.
