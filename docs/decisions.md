# Decisions for the next update (for your review before any code)

Every decision below points to entries in `docs/evidence/` (committed before this file). Evidence strength:
**Strong** = several RCTs / meta-analyses agree · **Moderate** = meta-analysis with limits, or consistent
smaller studies · **Weak** = small or indirect studies · **Expert opinion** = clinician consensus or
narrative review · **Judgment** = no direct evidence; a practical choice, labelled as such in the app.

## 1. Strength training: how much and how heavy

**What the evidence says.** For runners, the benefits on running economy and performance come from
**heavy loads (≥ 80% 1RM, ≈ 7 or fewer reps possible)** and from **combining heavy work with plyometrics**;
light/moderate-load work alone did not show a significant economy effect (strength-dose S1, S2). More
weekly sets give a bit more strength (S5: medium 5–9 > low ≤ 5 sets/exercise/week, a small difference),
single hard sets still work (S6, S7), and 1 session/week with 1 set at maintained load keeps strength
(S8). Strength training cut injuries across sports (S10), but a low-dose self-directed programme didn't in
first-time marathoners (S11).

**Your current routine** (2 × 12 in Phase 1, 3 × 6 in Phase 2, 1–2 sets once a week in Phase 3) is not
"doing nothing": 2 × 12 twice a week builds strength in people new to lifting (ACSM 8–12 RM for novices,
S9). But Phase 1 sits in the light/moderate range that showed no economy effect in runners, and its volume
is low (≈ 4 sets per exercise per week).

**Decisions**
- **Phase 1 (to Dec 13):** main lifts **3 × 8–10** (was 2 × 12), smaller lifts 2 sets, twice a week →
  6 weekly sets on the main lifts (S5 medium range; S9 novice range, moving toward heavier). *Moderate.*
- **Phase 2 (Dec 14 – Feb 7):** main lifts **3 × 5–6, heavy (about 2 reps left)** — the ≥ 80% 1RM range
  (S1, S2). Unchanged in spirit from the plan's 3 × 6. *Moderate.*
- **Add a small plyometric block from Phase 2** (e.g. 3 × 10 pogo hops, then single-leg hops), because
  combined heavy + plyometric training had the largest effects (S1, S2), and in a sub-group analysis
  plyometrics helped economy at speeds ≤ 12 km/h — your paces are ≈ 9.2–9.4 km/h (S1). **Only once your calf meets its hop test**
  (section 4). *Moderate (effect) / Judgment (exact sets).*
- **Phase 3 (Feb 8 – Apr 5):** keep once a week, heavy, 2 sets main lifts / 1 set others — matches the
  maintenance evidence (S8). *Moderate.*
- Progression engine unchanged (ACSM 2–10% rule, S9).

## 3. Stretching

**What the evidence says.** No stretch has been shown to prevent running injuries: Cochrane (6 trials,
RR 0.85, CI 0.65–1.12), a 1538-person RCT that stretched six major leg muscle groups every session, a
runner RCT, and two meta-analyses (stretching P1–P5). Stretching does not reduce soreness (P10). It does increase range of
motion — and strength training through full range does so about as well (P6, P9). Long static holds
(≥ 60 s) right before effort slightly reduce performance; short holds or dynamic movement don't (P6, P7).
Ten weeks of regular stretching didn't harm running economy (P8).

**Decisions**
- **Be honest in the app:** stretching is shown as **optional mobility** for comfort and range of motion,
  not injury prevention. *Strong (that it doesn't prevent injury).*
- **Keep the daily quad stretch** (your plan) and **add two calf stretches**: wall lean with **knee
  straight** (gastrocnemius) and **knee bent** (soleus) — both used in the calf-strain protocol (calf K2,
  phase 3). Still one daily "Stretch" quest with its XP.
- **Timing rule:** long holds (60–90 s) **after** runs or in the evening; if you stretch before a run, keep
  each hold under 60 s (P6, P7). *Moderate.*
- **While your calf is in its early stage, the calf stretches are paused** (section 4). *Expert opinion.*

## 4. Calf guidance (you decide; the app guides)

**What the evidence says.** No randomized trials exist for calf-strain rehab; the guidance is expert
consensus and narrative reviews (calf K1–K3). A soleus strain typically starts as "tightness" that doesn't
resolve (K1) — consistent with what you describe, though the app can't diagnose it. Rehab is staged:
isometric holds → single-leg calf raises (small range → full range, knee straight and bent) → loaded
raises → hops → running (K1, K2, K3). The reviews disagree on early stretching (K2: none in phase 1;
K3: light) and K2 advises no massage in the first days. Readiness to run: strength, hopping capacity and no
other signs (K1); one expert's rule: 20 single-leg hops without symptoms (K1). Full return: **≥ 30
single-leg calf raises and ≤ 10% difference between legs** (K1 checklist; K2 < 10%). Running early:
alternate days, no volume-and-intensity increase on consecutive days, strength work after running, avoid
long slow runs early (K1). Previous calf strain is the strongest predictor of the next one (K4).

**Decisions**
- **A "Calf" card** on Today, switched on when you flag your calf (and off when you clear it). It shows
  your current stage and that day's calf exercises, which replace the routine's calf raises:
  - **Stage 1 – Settle:** isometric single-leg holds, a few times a day; no calf stretching; no hard
    rolling on the sore spot (K2). Move on when walking and the holds are pain-free (K2).
  - **Stage 2 – Rebuild:** single-leg calf raises, knee straight and bent, small range → full range,
    daily (K1 "multiple loading bouts per day"). Move on when full-range single-leg raises are pain-free.
  - **Stage 3 – Strengthen:** dumbbell single-leg raises both ways (the routine's own), wall-lean calf
    stretches return (K2 phase 3), then pogo / single-leg hops. Move on at **20 single-leg hops without
    symptoms** (K1).
  - **Stage 4 – Return:** **≥ 30 single-leg raises each leg, ≤ 10% difference** (K1, K2).
  - Exact hold times and sets are **app defaults, labelled "not from a study"** (the sources give stages,
    not numbers). *Expert opinion / Judgment.*
- **Calf test log:** you record single-leg calf-raise counts for each leg (and hops); the app shows the
  difference and your progress toward 30 reps / ≤ 10%. *Expert opinion.*
- **Run-day guidance, not automatic changes:** while flagged, the run card shows the experts' return rules
  (alternate days, shorter, lift after running, don't add distance and pace on back-to-back days). You
  choose to run, shorten, or log it as missed with "Pain or injury" — the plan only adapts through your
  logs, as now. *Expert opinion.*

## 5. Easy runs: pace vs effort vs heart rate

**What the evidence says.** "Easy" means below the first ventilatory threshold, and the **talk test**
finds it: comfortable full sentences = below it (easy-intensity E1–E3). That threshold sits at a high and
individual %HRmax (≈ 82–90% in E1/E4), so fixed %HRmax rules are only averages (E8). Wrist HR is accurate
enough for steady running (E9, E10). Keeping ~70–80% of running easy improved performance more (E5–E7).
The plan's 11:45–13:00 /mi easy range comes from a coaching table, not from a test on you — and your
~10:30 easy pace overlaps the plan's tempo range for a 2:20 half (speed-work log). Either your fitness is
better than the plan assumed, or those runs are harder than easy; pace alone can't tell which.

**Decisions**
- **Easy is judged by effort first:** talk test (full sentences) and effort 3–4/10 (the plan's own
  definition). *Moderate.*
- **Add average heart rate** to run logging (from your Garmin). *Moderate (accuracy).*
- **Personal easy heart-rate cap (optional):** the app suggests calibrating it on a run where the talk test
  says "easy" and you note the average HR; you can also type one in. No age formula is used to set it
  (E1, E4, E8). *Moderate / Judgment (calibration method).*
- **Feedback changes:** an easy run faster than 11:45 /mi is **no longer flagged as "too fast"** when your
  effort is 3–4 and your HR is under your cap. It's flagged only when effort is 5+ or HR is above the cap.
  The pace range stays visible as a guide. *Moderate.*
- **Pace zones are not changed now.** Your March 7 half still resets marathon pace, as the plan says.
  The app shows a note that your easy pace overlaps the plan's 2:20 tempo range. *Judgment.*
